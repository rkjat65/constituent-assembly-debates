Indian States & the Union

Sovereignty · representation · negotiated entryDecember 1946 → April 1947
Theme · federal formation

How did hundreds of princely States become participants in one constitution-making process?

The early Assembly had to solve several problems at once: who represented the people of the States, whether participation could be compelled, how State institutions would relate to a future Union, and how representatives could enter the Assembly before every political question had been settled. The answer emerged incrementally through plenary argument, negotiation and committee machinery.

Argument in sequence

16 DEC 1946

Can the Assembly proceed without wider participation?

The Objectives Resolution debate makes the absence of the Muslim League and many Indian States a question of legitimacy and timing. Members disagree over whether delay would improve representativeness or merely paralyse the Assembly.

18 DEC 1946

Where does sovereignty lie inside the States?

The debate moves beyond rulers and governments toward the political standing of the people living in princely States. Representation and popular authority become linked rather than treated as separate questions.

21 DEC 1946

A negotiating mechanism is built

The Assembly creates a States negotiating mechanism so the problem can move from abstract sovereignty to practical questions such as seats and the method of returning representatives.

22 JAN 1947

The States thread broadens

The States negotiating machinery receives additional work around Bhutan and Sikkim while the Assembly continues building the institutional framework that will absorb future State participation.

28 APR 1947

Representatives of States actually enter the chamber

Sixteen representatives are attending. The House records that eleven are elected and five nominated. Their speeches insist that joining the Assembly is not a favour from British India but participation in a common national constitutional project.

Open Volume III sitting →
28 APR 1947

The compromise becomes institutional

Nehru reports the negotiations: up to 93 States seats, an agreed method of distribution and a compromise under which at least half the representatives should come through electoral mechanisms. State representatives also begin entering the Steering Committee.
