Rules & Procedure

Institution-building9–12 December 1946
Theme · institutional design

Before debating rights and federalism, the Assembly had to create a working constitutional forum.

The opening sittings established a temporary Chair, a process for electing the permanent President, interim procedural rules and a Rules Committee. These apparently technical decisions determined how the rest of constitution-making could proceed.

Opening chairDr. Sachchidananda Sinha
Key procedural voiceJ. B. Kripalani
Major transitionRajendra Prasad elected 11 Dec 1946

Institution-building in sequence

09 DEC

A temporary Chairman opens the Assembly

Kripalani invites Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha to take the Chair. The Assembly begins operating before its permanent presiding structure is in place.

10 DEC

Election rules and interim procedure

Kripalani moves the procedure for electing the permanent Chairman; Nehru proposes temporary use of Central Legislative Assembly rules while the Constituent Assembly frames its own.

10 DEC

A Rules Committee is constituted

The Assembly debates the committee's scope and the rules needed for the Assembly, its Sections and Committees.

11 DEC

Rajendra Prasad is declared elected

With valid nominations before the House and no competing valid nomination, Sinha declares Rajendra Prasad duly elected permanent Chairman.

12 DEC

Procedure shapes substantive debate

When members say they need more time to study the Objectives Resolution, the Chair agrees to postpone discussion rather than force immediate debate.

Why this matters

The early Assembly is already demonstrating a constitutional principle in practice: authority depends not only on outcomes, but on agreed procedures, notice, deliberation and a presiding structure accepted by the House.
