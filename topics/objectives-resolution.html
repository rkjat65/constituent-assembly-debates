Objectives Resolution

Constitutional aimsDecember 1946 → January 1947
Theme · constitutional programme

Before drafting Articles, the Assembly argued about what the Constitution was for.

Nehru's Objectives Resolution placed independence, popular authority, justice, equality, freedoms, territorial integrity and safeguards into one political programme. The debate that followed exposed disagreements over timing, representation and the meaning of the future Union.

Argument in sequence

12 DEC 1946

Discussion is postponed

Rajendra Prasad notes that members want more time to consider the important Resolution; the House agrees to take it up the next day.

13 DEC 1946

Nehru moves the Resolution

The Assembly is asked to declare its direction before proceeding deeper into constitution-making: the political community, source of authority and constitutional aims are set out at a high level.

16 DEC 1946

Postpone or proceed?

Jayakar's amendment turns the debate toward competence, timing, the Muslim League, the Indian States and whether wider participation requires delay.

17 DEC 1946

Competing paths to unity

Members argue over accommodation, political legitimacy, minority safeguards and whether present divisions should stop the Assembly from moving forward.

18 DEC 1946

Rules, minorities and the States

The debate expands into Assembly procedure, grouping, minority claims and the source of sovereignty inside the princely States.
