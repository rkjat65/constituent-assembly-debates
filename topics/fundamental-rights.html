Theme · rights architecture

A right becomes constitutional only after the Assembly answers four different questions.

What liberty or equality is being promised? Is the claim enforceable in a court? What restrictions may the State impose? And what remedy exists when public power violates the guarantee? The archive follows those questions separately so the evolution of the rights chapter remains visible.

Four analytical lenses

1 · Substance2 · Enforceability3 · Restrictions4 · Remedies

Substance: what liberty, equality or protection is promised? Enforceability: may a citizen compel compliance through a court? Restrictions: what public interests justify limiting the right? Remedies: which institution can make the guarantee effective?

Keeping these layers separate prevents a common reading error: treating every constitutional aspiration as if it had the same legal status and enforcement mechanism.

The social question underneath legal equality

The April debate makes clear that formal wording cannot be separated from social structure. P. R. Thakur links untouchability to caste, while other members test economic inequality, freedom of movement, access to trade and the practical ability of poor citizens to use constitutional remedies.
