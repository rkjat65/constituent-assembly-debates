G. Durgabai Deshmukh

Eminent advocate of the Madras High Court, social reformer, and founder of Andhra Mahila Sabha. The only woman member of the Constituent Assembly's Steering Committee and Rules Committee, Durgabai was an exceptionally active debater who intervened on judicial independence, the appointment and salaries of High Court and Supreme Court judges, universal adult franchise, national language, and women's property rights.

Indexed trajectory & key interventions

DEC 1946

Steering Committee and Parliamentary Procedure

Elected to the Steering Committee, ensuring smooth procedural coordination between the various expert committees, chairpersons, and the plenary floor.

View Steering Committee →
MAY–JUNE 1949

Defending Judicial Independence & Selection of Judges

Vigorously debated Articles 124 and 217 on the appointment of judges, arguing that the Chief Justice of India must be consulted and that judicial tenure must be secure to prevent political subservience.

Explore Judiciary provisions →
SEP 1949

Intervention on the Official Language Question

Delivered an impassioned plea on the Munshi-Ayyangar language compromise, urging northern advocates to show patience and goodwill towards non-Hindi regions during the transition period.

NOV 1949

Closing Assessment on Social Welfare & Adult Suffrage

Hailed the introduction of universal adult franchise as a radical act of faith in the Indian people, especially rural and underprivileged women.

How to read this personality

Durgabai combined razor-sharp legal acumen with grassroots social conviction. Unlike many members who spoke in abstract political terms, her interventions drilled down into institutional nuts and bolts: judicial salary scales, court jurisdiction, and practical enforcement mechanisms.

Historical claims remain tied to the dated debate records.
