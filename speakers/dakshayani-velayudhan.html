Dakshayani Velayudhan

Elected from the Madras constituency at age 34, Dakshayani Velayudhan was the first and only Dalit woman in the Constituent Assembly. A fierce orator and staunch egalitarian, she argued that the Constitution must be a living moral covenant that abolishes social inequality, refuses patronising separate electorates, and establishes universal human dignity through direct democratic citizenship.

Indexed trajectory & key interventions

19 DEC 1946

The Constitution as a New Framework of Life

In her electrifying maiden speech, she cautions against mechanically copying foreign constitutions and asserts that independent India must frame an original moral document rooted in social justice and emancipation of the oppressed.

29 APR 1947

Abolition of Untouchability & Social Enforceability

Participates in the debate on Clause 6 (later Article 17), insisting that the constitutional ban on untouchability must be accompanied by stringent legal penalties and active state duty to eliminate caste discrimination.

Open 29 April sitting →
AUG 1947 – 1949

Advocating Common Electorates and Universal Franchise

Argued courageously that true liberation for the Scheduled Castes lay in full integration into common democratic electorates, economic self-reliance, and universal education, rather than permanent segregation into statutory communal pockets.

How to read this personality

Dakshayani Velayudhan bridged anti-caste philosophy with universalist socialist republicanism. Her speeches consistently remind the Assembly that legal form without moral and social transformation would leave the subaltern citizen unprotected.
