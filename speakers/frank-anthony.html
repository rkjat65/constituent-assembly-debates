Frank Anthony

Distinguished barrister, parliamentarian, and the foremost leader of the Anglo-Indian community in the Constituent Assembly. Member of the Advisory Committee and Sub-Committee on Minorities, Anthony was an impassioned advocate for cultural and educational rights, drafting safeguards that matured into Article 29 and Article 30, and ensuring constitutional recognition for linguistic and religious minority schools.

Indexed trajectory & key interventions

19 DEC 1946

Supporting the Objectives Resolution on Equal Dignity

Intervenes during the Objectives Resolution debate, affirming that India's microscopic minorities place complete faith in the democratic idealism of the founding leaders while urging strong constitutional protections for minority cultures.

MAY 1947

Architecting Educational and Cultural Rights (Article 29 & 30)

Fights for the non-negotiable right of linguistic and religious minorities to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice without state discrimination in financial aid.

Open 1 May sitting →
AUG 1947 – 1949

Special Safeguards for the Anglo-Indian Community (Articles 331, 333, 336, 337)

Successfully negotiated transitional constitutional guarantees for Anglo-Indian legislative nomination and educational grants to protect vulnerable community schools.

25 NOV 1949

Final Assessment of the Constitution

In his closing speech, Anthony pays tribute to Dr. Ambedkar's monumental drafting achievement, expressing pride that India chose a composite, secular constitutional ethos over majoritarianism.

How to read this personality

Frank Anthony represented a tiny minority of less than two hundred thousand souls. His genius lay in framing minority rights not as communal separatism, but as an indispensable pillar of a modern, liberal, pluralistic democracy.
