Jaipal Singh Munda

Oxford scholar, Indian Olympic hockey captain (1928), and the pre-eminent voice representing thirty million Adivasis in the Constituent Assembly. Member of the Advisory Committee and the Sub-Committee on Tribal Areas, Jaipal Singh passionately defended tribal self-governance, customary land tenure, linguistic dignity, and constitutional safeguards that later crystallized in the Fifth and Sixth Schedules of the Indian Constitution.

Indexed trajectory & key interventions

19 DEC 1946

Welcome to the Free India: The Adivasi Standpoint

In one of the most memorable speeches of the Assembly, Jaipal Singh welcomed the Objectives Resolution while reminding the House that India's original inhabitants had suffered millennia of exploitation and needed genuine equality, respect, and constitutional protection.

24 JAN 1947

Structuring Tribal Representation in the Advisory Committee

Intervenes to ensure that indigenous representatives from Bihar, Assam, Central Provinces, and Orissa are included on the Advisory Sub-Committees investigating Tribal and Excluded Areas.

1947–1949

Architecting the Fifth & Sixth Schedules

Fought vigorously against patronising assimilation policies, advocating instead for tribal self-management, protection against land alienation, and autonomous district councils.

How to read this personality

Jaipal Singh rejected the term "backward classes" when applied to indigenous peoples, insisting on their proud identity as original inhabitants (Adivasis) with deeply democratic communal traditions that modern constitution-makers could learn from.
