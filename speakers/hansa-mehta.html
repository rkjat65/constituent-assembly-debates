Hansa Mehta

Eminent educationist, reformer, and one of the 15 women members of the Constituent Assembly. Member of the Sub-Committee on Fundamental Rights and the Advisory Committee. Hansa Mehta was a relentless champion of universal gender equality, non-discrimination in civil laws, the Uniform Civil Code, and presented the National Flag to the nation on midnight of 14 August 1947.

Sub-Committee on Fundamental Rights & Non-Discrimination

Advocated passionately that non-discrimination on grounds of sex must be explicit in Article 15 and Article 16, and championed the inclusion of the Uniform Civil Code as a crucial instrument for women's equal rights in marriage, inheritance, and guardianship.

1947–1948

International Legacy: Universal Declaration of Human Rights

Simultaneously serving on the UN Human Rights Commission alongside Eleanor Roosevelt, Hansa Mehta successfully changed the phrasing in Article 1 of the UDHR from "All men are born free and equal" to "All human beings are born free and equal."

How to read this personality

Hansa Mehta rejected the idea that women needed separate electorates or protectionist patronage. Her constitutional vision was grounded in unapologetic equality: that equal constitutional citizenship, universal adult franchise, and enforceable fundamental rights were the foundational prerequisites of Indian democracy.
