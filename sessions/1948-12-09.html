Religious Freedom and the Uniform Civil Code

9 December 194810:00 AMVolume VII · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

K. M. Munshi

Drafting Committee · Bombay

Delivers a masterly defence of secular nation-building, arguing that separating civil transactions and marriage from religion is essential to prevent permanent communal isolation.

View Munshi profile →
“
We are in a stage where we must unify and consolidate the nation by every means without interfering with religious practices. If however in past times, every aspect of life was given religious sanction, what were we doing? We are isolating communities into water-tight compartments... We want to divest religion from personal law, from what may be called social relations or from the rights of parties.
KM
— K. M. MunshiDebates on Secular Reform · 9 December 1948
⚖

Key principles settled during the 9 December sitting

  • 🕊Freedom of Conscience & Propagation: Draft Article 19 (Article 25) guaranteed all persons the right freely to profess, practise, and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality, and health.
  • 📜The Uniform Civil Code Objective: Draft Article 35 (Article 44) placed a constitutional directive upon the State to endeavour to secure for citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.
  • 🏛Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar's Intervention: Sir Alladi highlighted that vast areas of civil law (contract, evidence, property transfer) were already uniform across India, and codifying civil law would strengthen national fraternity without impairing religious faith.

✎ Right to "Propagate"

Lakshmikant Maitra and Christian delegates explained that "propagate" means the peaceful transmission of spiritual beliefs, not forcible conversion or coercion.

⚄ Article 44 Status

The provision was placed in Part IV (Directive Principles of State Policy) to allow democratic consensus and gradual parliamentary evolution.

K. M. Munshi on National Consolidation

1 K. M. Munshi: "There is one important consideration which we cannot afford to forget. Does religion cover every aspect of human life? There is no doubt that in a secular State, religion must be restricted to spheres which legitimately appertain to religion, and the rest of life must be regulated and modified for a consolidated nation."

Alladi Krishnaswami on Uniform Civil Laws

2 Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar: "A civil code runs into every department of civil relations, to the law of succession, to the law of marriage and similar matters. How have we enacted our Civil Procedure Code, Indian Penal Code, and Law of Transfer? If you look at those codes, they apply to all communities alike."

Dr. Ambedkar's Democratic Reassurance

3 Dr. B. R. Ambedkar: "The article merely proposes that the State shall endeavour for a uniform civil code. It does not say that the State shall immediately enforce it upon all citizens whether they want it or not... No government would exercise its power in such a manner as to provoke the resistance of any community."
