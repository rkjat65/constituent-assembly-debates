Adoption of the National Flag of India

22 July 194710:00 AMVolume IV · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Jawaharlal Nehru

Leader of the House · United Provinces

Moves the historic National Flag resolution, unfurling two silk and cotton flags before the Assembly and explaining the profound ethical symbolism of the Ashoka Chakra.

View Nehru profile →
“
Resolved that the National Flag of India shall be horizontal tricolour of deep Saffron (Kesari), White and dark Green in equal proportion... and in the centre of the white band, the wheel in navy blue to represent the Charkha... being the spinning wheel that appears on the abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital of Asoka.
JN
— Jawaharlal NehruFlag Resolution Motion · 22 July 1947
☸

Key dimensions of the National Flag debate

  • 📜The Wheel of the Law (Dharma Chakra): Nehru explained why the wheel from Asoka’s Sarnath pillar was chosen—honouring both Mahatma Gandhi’s spinning charkha and the ancient sovereign spirit of peace, ethical rule, and international brotherhood.
  • ✦Sarojini Naidu's Oratory: The Nightingale of India hailed the flag: "Remember, under this flag, there is no prince and there is no peasant, there is no rich and there is no poor. There is only duty, responsibility and sacrifice."
  • ⚖Universal Pluralism: Speeches by Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, Frank Anthony, Jaipal Singh, and Chaudhri Khaliquzzaman reaffirmed that the Flag belongs to every community, race, tribe, and creed across India.
  • ✔Standing Acclamation: The resolution was passed unanimously with all members standing in reverence to the newly consecrated National Flag.

✎ Sarnath Lion Capital Connection

The 24-spoke wheel is the Dharma Chakra from the Mauryan Emperor Asoka's Lion Capital at Sarnath, symbolizing perpetual motion, righteousness, and truth.

⚄ Women of India's Presentation

On the midnight of 14 August 1947, Hansa Mehta on behalf of the women of India formally presented this very National Flag to the Constituent Assembly.

Resolution Moved

1 Jawaharlal Nehru rises and unrolls two flags before the House: "Mr. President, it is my proud privilege on behalf of the Committee to place this resolution before the House... The Flag is of horizontal tricolour of deep saffron (kesari), white and dark green in equal proportion... with the Asoka wheel in navy blue."

The Meaning of Asoka's Wheel

2 "For my part, I am exceedingly happy that we have associated with our Flag not only some symbol from our past, but something which is also associated with the name of Asoka, one of the most magnificent names not only in India's history but in world history... It represents the continuous movement of time, the dynamic spirit of life, and the eternal principle of Dharma."

Dr. Radhakrishnan's Philosophy

3 Dr. S. Radhakrishnan: "The saffron colour denotes renunciation or disinterestedness... The white in the centre is light, the path of truth to guide our conduct. The green shows our relation to the soil, our relation to the plant life here, on which all other life depends. The Ashoka Wheel in the centre is the wheel of the law of dharma. Truth or satya, dharma or virtue, ought to be the controlling principles of all who work under this flag."

Unanimous Acclamation

4 Dr. Rajendra Prasad: "I would ask Members to stand up in their places and adopt the resolution." The whole Assembly rose in unison, passing the National Flag resolution amidst cheers that shook the hall.

A Flag of Sovereign Equality and Peace

Adopted on 22 July 1947, the National Flag served as the beacon under which India stepped out from British imperial rule into free democratic nationhood on 15 August 1947.
