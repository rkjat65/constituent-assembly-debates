Abolition of Untouchability: Adoption of Draft Article 11 (Article 17)

29 November 194810:00 AMVolume VII · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Dakshayani Velayudhan

Member · Madras · Scheduled Castes

First Dalit woman in the Assembly, joins Scheduled Caste delegates and national leaders in hailing the constitutional obliteration of untouchability and social hierarchy.

This is a day of great rejoicing for the millions of down-trodden people in India. This clause does not merely confer a right on sixty millions of human beings; it delivers humanity in India from a curse that has disfigured our civilization for thousands of years.

✦

Significance of the 29 November 1948 sitting

  • ⚖Absolute Constitutional Prohibition: Draft Article 11 was enacted without a single dissenting voice, declaring that the enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability is a penal offence punishable by law.
  • 🕊Tribute to Mahatma Gandhi: As Vice-President Dr. H. C. Mookherjee put the clause to vote, members across all benches rose and burst into shouts of "Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai!"
  • ✊Direct Enforceability: Unlike ordinary civil rights, this right was made enforceable against private individuals as well as the State, securing social liberation in daily life.

✎ Untouchability Offences Act

Article 17 gave Parliament the direct mandate to pass the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955 (later the Protection of Civil Rights Act) and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act.

⚄ Unanimous Acclamation

The entire Assembly adopted the clause with prolonged cheers and table-thumping, creating one of the most emotional moments in the Assembly's history.

Draft Article 11 Moved

1 The Vice-President (Dr. H. C. Mookherjee): "We now take up article 11. The motion is: 'That article 11 stand part of the Constitution: "Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of "Untouchability" shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.'"

S. Nagappa's Address

2 S. Nagappa: "Sir, this article provides the real emancipation of one-fifth of the total population of this country... We must see that this article is implemented in spirit and letter, so that every citizen can hold their head high with equal self-respect."

Unanimous Passage & Acclamation

3 The Vice-President put the question to the House. The motion was adopted unanimously amidst prolonged cheers and chants of Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai.
