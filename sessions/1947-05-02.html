Constitutional Remedies & Rights Report Adopted

2 May 19478:30 AMVolume III · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

K. M. Munshi

Member · Drafting Committee

Munshi vigorously defends the direct constitutional guarantee of prerogative writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, and Certiorari) as the irreplaceable bulwark against administrative despotism.

“
The right to move the Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings for the enforcement of the rights provided by this Part is guaranteed.
⚖
— Right to Constitutional RemediesClause 22 · 2 May 1947
☸

The four milestones of 2 May 1947

  • ⚖Guaranteed Judicial Remedies: Clause 22 (later Article 32) guarantees direct access to the Supreme Court for rights violations, empowering the judiciary with writ jurisdiction.
  • ⚔Armed Forces Exception: Parliament is granted explicit power to modify the application of fundamental rights to members of the armed forces to ensure discipline and national defence.
  • ⚑Advisory Committee Next Mandate: The House remits deferred minority, tribal, and fundamental principles clauses back to Patel's Advisory Committee for further consensus-building.
  • ✔Formal Adoption of Interim Report: The Constituent Assembly formally adopts the Interim Report on Fundamental Rights as amended, providing the bedrock for the future Drafting Committee.

✎ The Soul of the Constitution

Without Article 32, fundamental rights would be mere political declarations. By enshrining the right to remedies as a fundamental right itself, the Assembly created an unbreachable shield for every Indian citizen.

See Clause 22 debate ↓

⚄ Volume III Concludes

Volume III closes with fundamental rights entrenched into the constitutional structure. The Assembly adjourns until July 1947 to allow the Union Constitution Committee and Provincial Constitution Committee to draft their reports.

Right to Remedies

1 The Assembly takes up Clause 22. K. M. Munshi and Dr. B. R. Ambedkar emphasize that writs of Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, and Certiorari must be specifically mentioned so citizens have immediate legal weapons against unlawful state detention or administrative overreach.

Suspension in Emergency

2 Members scrutinize under what conditions the writ of Habeas Corpus may be suspended. The House accepts that suspension can only occur in grave emergencies of rebellion or foreign invasion as authorized by law.

Armed Forces

3 A special proviso is debated and added to permit the Union legislature to adapt the scope of fundamental rights for defence and police personnel to maintain discipline in national security services.

Referred Clauses

4 Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel summarizes the debate and formally moves that Clauses 16 (religious conversion of minors) and 17 (trade and commerce limitations) be remitted back to the Advisory Committee for subsequent reporting.

Report Adopted

5 Dr. Rajendra Prasad puts the substantive motion to the House: the Interim Report on Fundamental Rights, as debated and amended clause by clause over four days, is unanimously adopted by the Constituent Assembly.

Constitutional rights are now actionable law

By 2 May 1947, the Constituent Assembly moved from vague philosophical aspirations to an ironclad constitutional framework where individual liberties, equality, and enforceable remedies form the core of Indian republicanism.
