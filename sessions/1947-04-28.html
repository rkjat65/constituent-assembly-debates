The States Enter the Assembly

28 April 1947Volume III · opening sittingConstitution Hall, New Delhi

Jawaharlal Nehru

Chair · States Negotiating Committee

The States negotiations move from committee work into the Assembly itself. Nehru explains the compromise on representation, limits of the negotiating mandate and why joining the Union-making process is voluntary but consequential.

Why this sitting changes the archive

  • ◉The States become visible participants: representatives from several princely States present credentials and begin speaking inside the Assembly.
  • ◷The clock accelerates: the President links Britain's announced transfer deadline to a much tighter constitution-making timetable.
  • ⌁Federal design remains provisional: the Union-subject report is presented but deliberately not debated because the political settlement may still change the Centre–unit relationship.
  • ▦Institutional machinery expands: State representatives begin entering the Steering Committee rather than remaining outside the Assembly's working structure.

✎ Representation is already contested

The sitting distinguishes elected States representatives from nominated ones. That distinction matters because participation alone did not settle the democratic legitimacy of princely-State representation.

⚄ Federalism is tied to political uncertainty

Ayyangar does not treat the Union-subject report as final. League participation, fuller States representation and possible territorial division could all force the committee to reopen basic assumptions about Union and unit powers.
