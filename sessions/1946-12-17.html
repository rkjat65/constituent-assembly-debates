Ambedkar on Unity and the Beginning

17 December 194611:00 AMConstitution Hall, New Delhi

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Member · Constituent Assembly

Speaking on the Objectives Resolution

♙  View Speaker Profile
“
Our difficulty is not with regard to the ultimate, our difficulty is with regard to the beginning.
♙
— Dr. B. R. AmbedkarObjectives Resolution debate · 17 December 1946
⚖

Core of Ambedkar's intervention

  • ✺He distinguishes the broadly shared constitutional goals from the controversial political path to reach them.
  • ◉He expresses confidence that India's divided social and political groups can ultimately become a united people.
  • ⌁His emphasis is on making the beginning inclusive enough for absent or reluctant groups to join.
  • ♧He supports another effort at accommodation rather than treating the dispute as a narrow question of legal entitlement.

Goals

“ He separates broadly non-controversial constitutional objectives from the politically contested opening clauses.

Unity

“ He argues that present divisions do not make eventual national unity impossible.

Appeal

“ He urges statesmanship and accommodation so that those outside the process can be brought into it.
