Clause-by-Clause Consideration: Article 1 ("Union of States")

15 November 194810:00 AMVolume VII · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Drafting Committee Chairman · Bombay

Defends the structural distinction between a "Federation" and a "Union of States", asserting the indestructible unity of India and replying to Prof. K. T. Shah's amendments.

View Ambedkar profile →
“
India shall be a Union of States. The federation was not the result of an agreement by the States to join in a federation, and the federation not being the result of an agreement, no State has the right to secede from it. The Federation is a Union because it is indestructible. Though the country and the people may be divided into different States for convenience of administration, the country is one integral whole.
BA
— Dr. B. R. AmbedkarDraft Article 1 Defense · 15 November 1948
⚖

Crucial themes of the Clause 1 debate

  • 🌐"Union" vs. "Federation": Prof. K. T. Shah and others moved amendments to substitute "Secular, Socialist, Sovereign Democratic Republic" or "Federation". Ambedkar explained that "Union" was purposefully selected to signify that the Indian federation is indestructible and no constituent unit has a constitutional right to secession.
  • 📜The Name of the Nation: Members debated the dual terminology "India" and "Bharat". The synthesis "India, that is Bharat" acknowledged both ancient historical civilizational continuity and contemporary international treaty standing.
  • 🏛Democratic Flexibility: Ambedkar emphasized that the Constitution sets up a flexible federal system—capable of functioning as a federal structure in peacetime, yet seamlessly unified during emergencies.

✎ Indestructible Union

Unlike the United States where states existed before the union and negotiated a compact, Indian provinces and acceding princely states were integrated into one sovereign republic.

⚄ Article 1 Final Text

"1. (1) India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States. (2) The States and the territories thereof shall be as specified in the First Schedule."

Prof. K. T. Shah Moves Amendment

1 Prof. K. T. Shah: "I beg to move: 'That in clause (1) of article 1, after the words "India shall be a" the words "Secular, Socialist, Sovereign, Democratic Republic" be inserted.' Sir, I know that the words I propose to add may be regarded by some as superfluous... but I submit they are fundamental to our purpose."

Dr. Ambedkar's Famous Rebuttal

2 Dr. B. R. Ambedkar: "Sir, I regret that I cannot accept the amendment of Prof. K. T. Shah. My objections are twofold. First, what should be the policy of the State, how the Society should be organised in its social and economic side are matters which must be decided by the people themselves according to time and circumstances. It cannot be laid down in the Constitution itself, because that is destroying democracy altogether."

Indestructible Union of States

3 Dr. Ambedkar: "The Drafting Committee wanted to make it clear that though India was to be a federation, the federation was not the result of an agreement by the States... and that no State has the right to secede from it. The Federation is a Union because it is indestructible."
