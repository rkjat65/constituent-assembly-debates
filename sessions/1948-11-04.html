Dr. Ambedkar Introduces the Draft Constitution

4 November 194810:00 AMVolume VII · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Chairman · Drafting Committee

Moves the Draft Constitution for consideration in an epochal address expounding the parliamentary executive, federalism, fundamental rights, and the necessity of constitutional morality.

⚖

Five core doctrines of the Draft Constitution address

  • 🏛Parliamentary vs Presidential System: Ambedkar explains that the Draft prefers the British parliamentary model over the American presidential model because an infant democracy requires daily executive accountability alongside periodic stability.
  • 🌐Flexible Federalism: The Draft creates a dual polity with a single integrated judiciary, uniform basic civil/criminal law, and common All-India Services to withstand fissiparous tendencies.
  • ⚖Fundamental Rights & State Power: Defends reasonable restrictions as inherent to all organized societies, contrasting Indian provisions with American judicial police-power doctrines.
  • ☸Directive Principles of State Policy: Clarifies that Directive Principles establish economic democracy and bind future governments regardless of their party ideology.
  • 📜Constitutional Morality: Warns that democracy cannot survive on constitutional text alone; citizens and institutions must actively cultivate constitutional morality and reject unconstitutional agitation.

✎ The Magna Carta of Indian Democracy

Ambedkar's 4 November 1948 address is universally recognized as the foundational treatise on the philosophy, institutional design, and moral imperatives of the Indian Constitution.

Presentation

1 Dr. B. R. Ambedkar rises to move: "That the Constituent Assembly do proceed to take into consideration the Draft Constitution of India as settled by the Drafting Committee."

Parliamentary Executive

2 He articulates why the President is a constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Cabinet hold executive responsibility to the elected Lok Sabha, maximizing continuous day-to-day scrutiny over arbitrary one-man rule.

Federal Architecture

3 Explains how the Indian federation is an indestructible Union of destructible units, engineered to function as a federal system in normal times and convert into a unitary state during emergencies without formal constitutional amendment.

Village Panchayats & Individual

4 Answers critics who demanded making the village the basic unit of constitution, unapologetically placing the individual human citizen—not the caste-ridden village collective—as the foundational unit of the Constitution.

Constitutional Morality

5 Concludes with an impassioned plea to the nation: that the Constitution can only be as good as the men and women chosen to work it, urging adherence to constitutional methods rather than revolutionary violence or unthinking idol-worship.

The intellectual cornerstone of modern India

Ambedkar's introduction of the Draft Constitution on 4 November 1948 established the definitive philosophical rationale for India's constitutional democracy, parliamentary accountability, and social justice.
