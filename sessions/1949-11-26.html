The Constitution is Adopted & Enacted

26 November 194910:00 AMVolume XI · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Dr. Rajendra Prasad

President · Constituent Assembly

Delivers the comprehensive Presidential valedictory review of the Assembly's 3-year journey and puts the historic motion for the adoption and enactment of the Constitution of India.

The four pillars of the 26 November historic adoption

  • 📜Statistical Magnitude: The Assembly met for 11 sessions spanning 165 days over 2 years, 11 months, and 17 days, debating 2,473 amendments out of 7,635 tabled.
  • ⚖Tribute to Dr. Ambedkar: President Rajendra Prasad commends Dr. Ambedkar: "Sitting in the Chair and watching the proceedings from day to day, I have realised as no one else could have, with what zeal and devotion the members of the Drafting Committee and especially its Chairman, Dr. Ambedkar, have worked. They could not have made a better choice."
  • 🏛The Human Factor: Prasad observes that a constitution, like a machine, is a lifeless thing. It acquires life only through the men who control and operate it, emphasizing that India needs nothing more than a class of honest, dedicated men and women who place the country above themselves.
  • ✍Enactment Proclamation: "We, the People of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign Democratic Republic... in our Constituent Assembly this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution."

✎ Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)

26 November is celebrated across India as National Constitution Day, commemorating the moment when the people of India gave to themselves their supreme organic law.

See the adoption motion ↓

⚄ Immediate Enforcement of Key Clauses

While the major body of the Constitution took effect on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day), Article 394 brought into immediate force 15 crucial provisions regarding Citizenship (Articles 5–9), Elections (Article 324), and Provisional Parliament.

Presidential Review

1 Dr. Rajendra Prasad addresses the Assembly: "Before I put the motion to vote, I desire to say a few words. This is a historic occasion. The Constituent Assembly has had eleven sessions. The first session was held on 9th December 1946. The Constitution consists of 395 articles and 8 schedules."

The Chair Commends Ambedkar

2 "Sitting in the Chair and watching the proceedings from day to day, I have realised as no one else could have, with what zeal and devotion the members of the Drafting Committee and especially its Chairman, Dr. Ambedkar, in spite of his indifferent health, have worked. (Cheers). We could never make a decision which was or could be ever so right as when we put him on the Drafting Committee and made him its Chairman. He has not only justified his selection but has added lustre to the work which he has done."

Character of the People

3 "A Constitution like a machine is a lifeless thing. It acquires life because of the men who control it and operate it, and India needs today nothing more than a set of honest men who will have the interest of the country before them. There is a fissiparous tendency in our country... If the people who are elected are capable and men of character and integrity, they would be able to make the best even of a defective Constitution. If they lack these, the Constitution cannot help the country."

Motion Put & Adopted

4 Dr. Rajendra Prasad puts the formal question: "The question is: That the Constitution as settled by the Assembly be passed." The motion was adopted amidst prolonged, deafening cheers from all quarters of the House.

Vande Mataram & Jana Gana Mana

5 Dr. Rajendra Prasad signs the authenticated copy of the Constitution. The Assembly members rise in unison as the national songs are sung, concluding the epic constitution-making labours and inaugurating the constitutional Republic of India.

We, the People of India

On 26 November 1949, the people of India gave to themselves a revolutionary charter of democratic sovereignty, social justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity, transforming a colonial dependency into the world's largest constitutional republic.
