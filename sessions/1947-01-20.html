Who Does the Assembly Represent?

Dr. Rajendra Prasad

Opens Volume II by defending the Assembly's representative character.

What changes in Volume II

  • ✺Legitimacy is argued explicitly: Rajendra Prasad answers British parliamentary attacks on the Assembly's composition.
  • ◉Machinery grows: the House elects a Steering Committee to order business and coordinate constitutional work.
  • ⌁The Objectives Resolution returns: Radhakrishnan, Gadgil, Vijayalakshmi Pandit, Ranga and others connect independence to rights, equality and social change.
  • ♧Rights become practical: Ranga asks how poor citizens will actually enforce constitutional guarantees.

✎ Representation is not a side issue

The first substantive statement of Volume II is about who is present, who is absent and whether those absences invalidate the Assembly's claim to act constitutionally.

Jump to opening statement ↓

⚄ Rights move from ideal to enforcement

The sitting does more than repeat abstract freedoms. Ranga presses the practical problem of whether poor citizens can reach courts and use the rights a constitution promises.
