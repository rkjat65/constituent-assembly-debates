The Grammar of Anarchy: Dr. Ambedkar's Final Address

25 November 194910:00 AMVolume XI · Constitution Hall, New Delhi

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Chairman · Drafting Committee

Delivers his closing reply to the Assembly, sharing credit with Sir B. N. Rau, S. N. Mukerjee, and his Drafting Committee colleagues while delivering three profound warnings for safeguarding the infant Republic.

View Ambedkar profile →
“
If we wish to maintain democracy not merely in form, but also in fact, what must we do? The first thing in my judgment we must do is to hold fast to constitutional methods of achieving our social and economic objectives... It means we must abandon the Grammar of Anarchy.
BA
— Dr. B. R. AmbedkarValedictory Address · 25 November 1949
☸

The three epochal warnings of 25 November 1949

  • ⚖Abandon the Grammar of Anarchy: Bloodless constitutional methods must replace unconstitutional agitations, civil disobedience, and satyagraha when legal methods of redress are open.
  • ✦Reject Hero-Worship (Bhakti): Quoting John Stuart Mill, Ambedkar cautions that no people should lay their liberties at the feet of even a great man. Bhakti in religion may lead to salvation, but in politics, Bhakti or hero-worship leads inevitably to degradation and dictatorship.
  • 🏛Make Political Democracy a Social Democracy: Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social democracy—a way of life which recognizes liberty, equality, and fraternity as inseparable principles of life.
  • 📜The Life of Contradictions: Warning that if social and economic inequality is not removed swiftly, those who suffer from inequality will blow up the structure of political democracy which the Assembly had so laboriously built.

✎ The Soul of Indian Constitutionalism

This address is universally cited by the Supreme Court of India and constitutional scholars as the moral compass of the Republic, defining the perpetual duty of citizens and courts to defend democratic equality.

⚄ S. N. Mukerjee & Sir B. N. Rau

In this sitting, Ambedkar generously acknowledges the extraordinary drafting craftsmanship of Chief Draughtsman S. N. Mukerjee and Constitutional Adviser Sir B. N. Rau.

Tribute to Colleagues

1 Dr. B. R. Ambedkar rises to close the debate: "The credit that is given to me does not really belong to me. It belongs partly to Sir B. N. Rau, the Constitutional Adviser... A part of the credit must also go to the Chief Draughtsman, Mr. S. N. Mukerjee. His ability to put the most intricate points into the simplest and clearest legal language cannot be easily surpassed."

The First Warning

2 "If we wish to maintain democracy not merely in form, but also in fact, what must we do? The first thing in my judgment we must do is to hold fast to constitutional methods of achieving our social and economic objectives. It means we must abandon the bloody methods of revolution. It means that we must abandon the method of civil disobedience, non-cooperation and satyagraha... These methods are nothing but the Grammar of Anarchy and the sooner they are abandoned, the better for us."

The Second Warning

3 "The second thing we must do is to observe the caution which John Stuart Mill has given to all who are interested in the maintenance of democracy, namely, not 'to lay their liberties at the feet of even a great man, or to trust him with powers which enable him to subvert their institutions.' There is nothing wrong in being grateful to great men who have rendered life-long services... But there are limits to gratefulness... In India, Bhakti or hero-worship plays a part in its politics unequalled in magnitude... In politics, Bhakti or hero-worship is a sure road to degradation and to eventual dictatorship."

The Third Warning

4 "The third thing we must do is not to be content with mere political democracy. We must make our political democracy a social democracy as well. Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social democracy. What does social democracy mean? It means a way of life which recognizes liberty, equality and fraternity as the principles of life. These principles of liberty, equality and fraternity are not to be treated as separate items in a trinity. They form a union of trinity in the sense that to divorce one from the other is to defeat the very purpose of democracy."

Life of Contradictions

5 "On the 26th of January 1950, we are going to enter into a life of contradictions. In politics we will have equality and in social and economic life we will have inequality. In politics we will be recognizing the principle of one man one vote and one vote one value. In our social and economic life, we shall, by reason of our social and economic structure, continue to deny the principle of one man one value. How long shall we continue to live this life of contradictions? How long shall we continue to deny equality in our social and economic life? If we continue to deny it for long, we will do so only by putting our political democracy in peril. We must remove this contradiction at the earliest possible moment or else those who suffer from inequality will blow up the structure of political democracy which this Assembly has so laboriously built up."

An eternal charter of democratic vigilance

Ambedkar's 25 November 1949 speech stands as the supreme intellectual and moral testament of the Constituent Assembly, reminding every generation of Indians that constitutional democracy is a living, continuous struggle for social fraternity and economic justice.
